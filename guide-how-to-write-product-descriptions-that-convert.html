How to Write Product Descriptions That Convert

Good product descriptions do one simple job: they help a customer decide if your item solves their problem. Most product pages fail because they list features instead of answering what the buyer actually wants to know. Here is a practical, no-nonsense framework to write product descriptions that sell.

Start with the primary benefit. When shoppers look at a product, their first question is "What is in it for me?" A feature is a fact about the item, like "printed on 100gsm recycled paper." A benefit is what that fact does for the buyer, like "prevents ink bleed-through so your notes stay clean." Always translate features into benefits.

Know your audience and speak their language. Avoid jargon unless your customers use it every day. If you sell woodworking tools, use the correct terminology. If you sell everyday planners, use clear, plain language about scheduling and daily habits. Read reviews of similar products to see the exact words buyers use to describe their frustrations and desires.

Keep formatting scannable. Most people do not read product descriptions word for word. They scan them. Break up text with short paragraphs. Use bullet points for key details like dimensions, materials, or included items. Put the most important information in the first two sentences so mobile users see it immediately.

Address objections upfront. Shoppers hesitate when they have unanswered questions. Will this fit my desk? Is it difficult to set up? Answer these common questions directly in the description. Clear details reduce returns and build trust.

End with a clear call to action. Tell the buyer exactly what to do next. Keep it simple and direct.
